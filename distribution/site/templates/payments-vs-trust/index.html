Payments are going production. Trust isn't.

2026-11-23

Agents can pay now.

A 402 response is no longer an exotic demo. It arrives with a price, a network, an asset, and a payee. An agent can read the challenge, sign a payment, and retry the request in seconds. On Base, the settlement is on-chain. On Lightning, a preimage proves an invoice was paid. The payment rails are becoming ordinary.

That is progress. It is also the moment the next question becomes unavoidable:

What did the payment actually prove?

A settlement is evidence, not a verdict

A settled payment proves that money moved from one address to another at a particular time. That is a real and useful fact. It is not the same as saying the counterparty will deliver, that the endpoint is honest, or that the customer was independent.

Payment data is silent about several things an agent needs:

  • whether the payer and the payee are separate entities;
  • whether the service behind the endpoint was delivered;
  • whether a seller's volume comes from customers or from wallets under its control;
  • whether the same operator is running the same business under a different address.

A system that treats "this address has settled payments" as "this address is trustworthy" has answered the easy question and skipped the hard one.

Unknown is not bad

There is a second failure, quieter than the first: treating missing evidence as negative evidence.

An address with no record may be new. It may be a payer rather than a seller. It may be on a rail the index does not cover. None of those facts justify a zero. A zero is a claim, and the data does not support it.

So the honest answers are separate:

  • scored — there is enough evidence to interpret, with a stated confidence and the rules that produced it;
  • insufficient evidence — some record exists, but it is too thin to interpret;
  • unknown — no record in this index;
  • rail not indexed — this rail is outside what the index can see.

Collapsing those into one number is convenient. It is also how an agent ends up refusing a legitimate new seller, or paying a familiar one without asking a second question.

What we are building

Zapproof is a read-only check that an agent can call before it pays:

What settled-payment evidence exists for this counterparty, and what does it not prove?

It is non-custodial. It does not hold funds, route payments, or sit in the payment path. It has no token and no way to buy standing. It reports observed settlements and a rules-based interpretation, and it says plainly when the evidence is too thin to interpret.

The current index covers Base x402 settlements. Lightning and MPP are not indexed. A Lightning query returns rail_not_indexed, which means exactly that: missing coverage, not a negative finding. We would rather say that than relabel Base activity as something it is not.

The limitation we publish with the product

The interpretation is not independently validated. In a blinded adjudication, language models agreed with it about 62% of the time, or 83% when their abstentions are excluded. They are not human reviewers, and the sample is small. Every API and MCP response says this. A number without its limitation travels badly.

That is not a reason to wait for a perfect classifier. It is a reason to use the answer as one input, cap exposure when evidence is thin, and keep a human in the loop for consequential payments.

The check belongs before the payment

The useful place for a reputation signal is not after a dispute. It is before the signature:

  1. The agent receives a 402 challenge.
  2. It checks the counterparty.
  3. It pays the full amount, pays a capped amount with approval, or stops.
  4. It records why.

That order costs one extra request. It also makes the agent's decision auditable, which matters more as agents begin to spend on their own.

Payments are becoming infrastructure. Trust has to become a check the agent can run, with its limits attached.

Check before you pay.
